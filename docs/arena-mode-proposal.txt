Frankentower — Arena Mode

Status: Feature proposal
Core concept: Endless asynchronous PvP using saved player builds
Design principle: Reuse the existing Frankentower game loop and combat systems. Arena Mode primarily replaces generated encounters with player-created opponents.

1. Overview

Arena Mode is an asynchronous competitive mode in which players progress through Frankentower normally, but fight saved builds created by other players instead of the standard generated encounters.

Players continue to:

* Shop for equipment.
* Equip and combine items.
* Modify their anatomy.
* Manage circulation and other build constraints.
* Fight using the existing autobattle system.
* Receive normal progression and rewards.
* Advance through successive days.

The principal difference is opponent selection.

In Tower Mode, the game selects or generates the encounter appropriate to the current floor.

In Arena Mode, the game selects a saved player build from the corresponding day.

Arena Mode has no predefined final day.

The furthest point reached by the player community becomes the current frontier. A player who reaches a day for which no opponent yet exists wins the current Arena run and establishes the first opponent for that new day.

The Arena therefore grows as players push its frontier forward.

⸻

2. Design goals

Arena Mode should:

* Provide effectively endless replayability.
* Make player-created monsters meaningful beyond their original run.
* Require minimal divergence from the existing Frankentower systems.
* Allow asynchronous competition without requiring players to be online together.
* Preserve the existing build, shop, surgery and combat experience.
* Produce encounters against strange and memorable builds that the game’s normal encounter generator could not create.
* Allow the community itself to determine how deep the Arena currently extends.
* Make reaching the frontier feel like a meaningful achievement.
* Continue functioning with a relatively small active player population.

Arena Mode should not become a separate competitive ruleset unless playtesting demonstrates that this is necessary.

⸻

3. Core loop

The Arena loop is:

1. Begin an Arena run.
2. Enter Day 1.
3. Prepare the creature using the existing shop, equipment and anatomy systems.
4. Save the player’s Day 1 build.
5. Select an opponent from saved Day 1 builds.
6. Fight using the normal combat engine.
7. Resolve the normal post-combat progression.
8. Advance to Day 2.
9. Repeat.

The game continues for as long as the player can continue winning and opponents exist.

There is no predetermined Day 20, Day 100 or other final encounter.

⸻

4. Day snapshots

Arena matchmaking operates on saved snapshots, not on another player’s current character.

A snapshot represents the exact combat-ready state of a creature on a particular Arena day.

A snapshot should contain everything required to reconstruct that combatant, including:

* Anatomy.
* Equipment.
* Item levels.
* Derived statistics.
* Relevant permanent effects.
* Appearance information.
* Player/creature display identity.
* Arena day.
* Rules/content version information required for compatibility.
* Any other state required by the combat engine.

Once submitted to the Arena pool, a snapshot does not change when its original player subsequently modifies their creature.

For example:

Alice reaches Day 14 and submits Agnes as a Day 14 snapshot.
Alice later reaches Day 21 and substantially changes Agnes.
Players matched against Day 14 Agnes still encounter the original Day 14 version.

This allows the Arena to retain a history of builds at every stage of progression.

⸻

5. Matchmaking

For an ordinary Arena day, the game selects an eligible saved player snapshot from that same day.

Basic rule

Day N players fight Day N opponents.

Do not initially attempt to calculate an abstract power rating and match creatures by estimated strength.

Differences in build effectiveness are part of the competitive game.

A player who discovers an exceptionally effective Day 12 build should create a difficult Day 12 opponent.

Opponent selection

Where multiple snapshots exist, matchmaking should select among them with enough randomness to prevent runs from becoming predictable.

Selection may later account for considerations such as:

* Avoiding the player’s own snapshots.
* Avoiding the same opponent repeatedly within one run.
* Providing reasonable variety between players.
* Reducing excessive repetition of extremely common builds.

These are matchmaking refinements rather than changes to combat balance.

⸻

6. The frontier

The defining Arena mechanic is the frontier.

The frontier is the highest Arena day currently populated by player snapshots.

Suppose the community has produced opponents through Day 23.

A player who defeats a Day 23 opponent advances to Day 24.

If no Day 24 opponent exists, that player has reached unexplored territory.

The game declares them an Arena winner.

Their Day 24 build is then saved as the first Day 24 opponent.

Day 24 has become part of the Arena.

The next player to reach Day 24 must fight a saved Day 24 build.

If they win and no Day 25 opponent exists, they become the first player to establish Day 25.

In this way, the Arena’s maximum depth emerges from player activity rather than from a developer-defined endpoint.

⸻

7. Frontier victory

Reaching an empty day should be presented as a significant achievement.

The player has not defeated a special scripted final boss. They have reached a point nobody has previously populated.

The victory presentation should communicate something equivalent to:

THE FRONTIER

No challenger has reached Day 37 before you.

Your creature now stands as the first Day 37 opponent.

The exact wording and presentation can follow the final Frankentower UI style.

The run may end at this point.

The newly submitted creature remains available for future challengers.

This creates an emergent competitive objective:

Reach the frontier, then become part of the obstacle preventing the next player from advancing beyond it.

⸻

8. Multiple opponents per day

Ordinary days should accumulate multiple player snapshots.

The Arena should not consist solely of one linear sequence of champions.

If 100 players reach Day 12, the Day 12 pool can contain builds from those runs.

This produces variety and prevents Arena Mode from becoming a fixed player-created campaign.

The frontier is unusual only because a newly established day initially contains a single build.

As additional players reach that day, its opponent pool naturally expands.

⸻

9. Snapshot submission timing

The player’s build should be captured at a consistent point in the day.

The preferred rule is:

Submit the combat-ready build immediately before its Arena fight.

This ensures that the opponent pool contains creatures representing the actual builds players chose to take into combat on that day.

The frontier case uses the build that would have entered combat if an opponent had existed.

Snapshot submission must not depend on winning the subsequent fight. Losing builds are still legitimate Arena opponents.

This is important for maintaining build diversity and preventing the pool from containing only successful meta builds.

⸻

10. Combat

Arena combat should use the existing Frankentower combat engine without special PvP combat rules.

Once combat begins, the engine should ideally not care whether an opponent originated from:

* A normal generated encounter.
* A scripted boss.
* A retired player boss.
* An Arena snapshot.

All should resolve through the same combatant representation wherever practical.

This reduces implementation complexity and prevents Arena Mode from developing a parallel combat system.

Where possible, Arena combat should be reproducible from saved combatants and the combat seed. This assists debugging and validation.

⸻

11. Progression and economy

Arena Mode should initially reuse the existing progression economy.

Do not create separate Arena versions of:

* Shop prices.
* Item rarity.
* Equipment levels.
* Combining.
* Surgery.
* Circulation.
* Anatomy rules.
* Combat rules.
* Reward progression.

The assumption should be that the existing game works unchanged until Arena playtesting proves otherwise.

If Arena-specific balance problems emerge, address the smallest necessary rule rather than immediately creating a parallel competitive ruleset.

The objective is for knowledge gained in Tower Mode to transfer directly into Arena Mode.

⸻

12. Low-population behaviour

Arena Mode must account for days with small opponent pools.

A newly populated day may have only one opponent.

That is intentional at the frontier.

For established days, repeated encounters with the same build may become undesirable.

Possible later mitigations include:

* Prefer opponents the player has not encountered during the current run.
* Weight selection toward less recently encountered snapshots.
* Maintain developer-created Arena snapshots for early days.
* Use suitable existing saved builds as fallback opponents.

The exact fallback strategy can depend on expected player population.

However, the game must preserve the fundamental distinction between:

No opponent exists because the player has reached the frontier

and:

The matchmaking pool exists but currently has limited variety.

The first condition is a victory. The second is not.

⸻

13. Player identity and privacy

Arena opponents should primarily be remembered as creatures, not accounts.

The encounter presentation should emphasise:

* Creature name.
* Appearance.
* Anatomy.
* Equipment/build.
* Arena day.

Displaying the creator’s account name is optional and should follow whatever identity/privacy model RUN.world supports.

A player should never need another player’s live presence or contact information to participate.

⸻

14. Arena history

Arena Mode creates several potentially interesting statistics without requiring them for the first implementation.

Examples include:

* Highest day reached.
* Number of frontier victories.
* Current Arena frontier.
* Number of challengers defeated by a submitted build.
* Most successful creature.
* Deepest creature snapshot.
* Builds responsible for extending the frontier.
* Number of unique opponents encountered.

A later Arena history screen could preserve notable frontier holders.

This should remain secondary to getting the core asynchronous matchmaking working.

⸻

15. Relationship to the Hall of Shame

Arena snapshots and retired bosses are related concepts but serve different purposes.

Hall of Shame / retired bosses

Represent notable completed creatures preserved as part of the player’s history and existing boss system.

Arena snapshots

Represent creatures captured at particular stages of active Arena progression and used for asynchronous matchmaking.

A single creature may therefore produce many Arena snapshots during one run.

The systems may share serialization and rendering infrastructure without becoming the same feature.

⸻

16. Rules and version compatibility

Because Arena opponents persist beyond the session in which they were created, saved snapshots must account for game updates.

A snapshot should identify the rules/content version under which it was created.

The first implementation does not need an elaborate migration system.

Reasonable strategies include:

* Only matching snapshots from compatible rules versions.
* Invalidating Arena pools after incompatible balance changes during development.
* Regenerating derived statistics from canonical saved build information where safe.

During the game jam and early development, clearing incompatible Arena data is preferable to maintaining complicated historical compatibility.

⸻

17. Abuse and validation

Player snapshots must not be trusted merely because they were submitted by a client.

Before an Arena build enters the matchmaking pool, the game should validate that its state is legal under the relevant game rules.

At minimum, validate:

* Anatomy configuration.
* Equipment ownership and placement.
* Item levels.
* Slot compatibility.
* Derived statistics where appropriate.
* Circulation/anatomy constraints.
* Day/progression state.
* Any economy-derived state required to prevent impossible builds.

The exact trust boundary depends on RUN.world’s architecture, but Arena Mode should be designed with the assumption that persisted competitive builds require validation.

⸻

18. UI requirements

Arena Mode should require relatively little new game UI.

At run start, allow the player to choose between:

Tower

Fight the tower’s normal encounters.

Arena

Fight creatures created by other players and try to reach the frontier.

During an Arena run, the existing shop, surgery, equipment and combat interfaces should remain substantially unchanged.

Before combat, the game may identify the opponent as a player-created Arena creature.

The HUD should display the current Arena day rather than implying a finite floor objective.

When the player reaches the frontier, show a dedicated victory presentation explaining that their current build has established the next Arena day.

The mode should not require a persistent leaderboard or social interface for its initial implementation.

⸻

19. Architectural intent

Arena Mode should be implemented as an alternative source of encounters rather than as a second copy of Frankentower.

Conceptually:

              Frankentower Run
                     |
        Shop / Surgery / Equipment
                     |
              Select Opponent
                /          \
               /            \
       Tower Encounter    Arena Snapshot
               \            /
                \          /
                Combat Engine
                     |
                  Outcome
                     |
                 Next Day

The implementation should preserve this separation.

The combat system receives a valid opponent.

It should not need substantial Arena-specific logic after that point.

⸻

20. Initial implementation scope

A minimal Arena Mode requires:

1. Arena/Tower mode selection.
2. Serialization of combat-ready player snapshots.
3. Submission of snapshots by Arena day.
4. Retrieval of eligible same-day snapshots.
5. Random opponent selection.
6. Prevention of obvious self-matching.
7. Existing combat against the retrieved snapshot.
8. Detection of an empty next-day pool.
9. Frontier victory.
10. Submission of the first snapshot for the new frontier day.
11. Basic compatibility/version handling.
12. Appropriate fallback/error behaviour when matchmaking services are unavailable.

Everything else—leaderboards, opponent records, frontier history, build popularity, rankings and sophisticated matchmaking—can follow later.

⸻

21. Success criteria

Arena Mode succeeds if:

* A Tower player can enter Arena Mode and immediately understand the existing game systems.
* Arena runs require almost no duplicated gameplay implementation.
* Player builds can be reliably reconstructed as opponents.
* Two creatures at the same day can fight using the existing combat engine.
* Arena days naturally accumulate a variety of opponents.
* Reaching an unpopulated day reliably establishes a new frontier.
* The frontier can advance indefinitely without requiring new developer-authored encounters.
* Seeing another player’s bizarre creature is itself an enjoyable part of reaching the next fight.

The central design test is simple:

Could Frankentower keep going forever if the developers never authored another enemy?

For Arena Mode, the answer should be yes.
